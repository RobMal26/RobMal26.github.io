About

I am a doctoral researcher in General Anthropology at the Faculty of Humanities, Charles University, Prague. For more than ten years I have kept returning to Roma settlements in the Spiš region, above all Letanovce. Since 2025 I have also been doing fieldwork in Sheffield and Rotherham, where many families from these settlements now live. My dissertation looks at what happens to people's skills, networks and standing when they migrate, and draws mainly on Pierre Bourdieu.

At Charles University I teach a seminar in academic skills and, with Ondřej Skripnik, run a fieldwork course that takes students to Letanovce. I also work with the Giľora association on documenting Romani music. I am a Sylff Fellow.

Talks and expertise

Each talk can be adapted for secondary school students, university audiences or practitioners.

Roma settlements in eastern Slovakia: why they are not all the same

Settlements differ in their history, their relations with the municipality, access to education and how many people leave to work abroad. Comparing Letanovce and Spišské Tomášovce, I show where these differences come from and why general talk about “the settlements” often misses how people actually live there.

For: schools and universities, NGOs, general audiences

From Spiš to Sheffield: Roma migration to Britain

Why Slovak Roma move to England, how they settle there and what migration gives and takes away. Based on fieldwork in Sheffield and Rotherham, including the role of Pentecostal churches and relations with other minorities in Page Hall.

For: universities, practitioners, organisations working with migrants

Family, visits and shared space

First developed for architecture students at the Czech Technical University. How Roma families use flats and houses, what hospitality and privacy mean to them, and why the assumptions of planners and housing officers sometimes miss this. The main case is the Janov housing estate in Litvínov.

For: architects, planners, municipalities and housing departments

Ethnographic fieldwork in practice

What long-term fieldwork involves, how to build relationships in the field, how to keep notes and how to turn them into readable writing. Drawn from my own research and from years of taking students into the field.

For: students in the humanities and social sciences, summer schools

Romani song as family memory

What Romani songs say about home, leaving and family ties, and how the Giľora association records them and brings them back to the communities they come from.

For: schools, cultural institutions, festivals

Talks usually run 45 to 90 minutes and can also take the form of a workshop or discussion. I speak English, Czech and Slovak.
